01 / City foundation models

Foundation models,
not digital twins.

The goal is a useful representation of a city. Not a replica of everything in it.

Our models are designed to learn relationships among buildings, climate, policy, and local conditions. They connect physical patterns with things that are related by meaning, purpose, or everyday experience.

Each model is locally designed. Its evidence, methods, and questions reflect a city’s particular physical environment, social fabric, and administrative needs.

02 / Forge

The system behind
the intelligence.

Forge builds the models—and develops the methods that make the next model better.

Our internal knowledge and development system connects agents, evidence, analytical knowledge, and accumulated experience. It gives model development a foundation that grows with the work.

  • Organise the knowledge. Bring research, methods, and experience into the development process.
  • Develop and test. Use agents and analyst expertise to investigate evidence and evaluate approaches.
  • Improve the methodology. Carry learning forward into the construction and updating of city models.

As Forge improves, updates can become increasingly autonomous. Analysts remain in the loop, and changes remain connected to their evidence and methodology.

03 / Core

Put models to work.
Keep the evidence.

Core serves city foundation models alongside BIT’s global and country-level indicators.

It is the operational interface for accessing and maintaining these resources. Core is deliberately evidence-preserving: it does not independently improve itself or replace the evidence beneath a result.

  • Lineage. Connect outputs to the data and transformations behind them.
  • Provenance. Retain where evidence came from and how it was used.
  • Reproducibility. Maintain methods, versions, and the underlying evidence.

Updates are traceable, with analysts responsible for oversight and interpretation.

04 / Helios

Energy intelligence,
inside and out.

In development / Pilot testing

A privacy-protecting, 360° view of a building.

Helios is our internal building-energy product. It uses on-device machine learning to extract useful features from LiDAR observations of interior spaces. The on-device workflow is designed to protect occupant privacy while producing structured spatial evidence for analysis.

For the outside view, Helios uses building and neighbourhood indicators served by Core. It can connect those features to a city foundation model where one is available, or work with broader indicators on their own.

A physics engine brings the interior and exterior evidence together to support whole-building energy analysis, retrofit planning, and property decisions.

Experimental / Kitchener–Waterloo

Mariposa.
Where we learn
what a city can tell us.

Our experimental city foundation model is grounded in Kitchener–Waterloo.

Its name is inspired by Stephen Leacock’s Mariposa: a small place in which, in a sense, everything happens. It is our testing ground for locally designed intelligence.

Mariposa is an active experiment.
